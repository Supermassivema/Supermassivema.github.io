The Australian Museum in Sydney has returned 17 skulls and one hair sample belonging to Rapa Nui ancestors, which the museum acquired in 1883 following a German naval expedition to the island the previous year. A handover ceremony took place in Sydney on 15 July 2026, attended by museum director Kim McKay and Carlos Edmunds Paoa, president of the Rapa Nui Council of Elders, with further ceremonies in Santiago two days later and on Rapa Nui itself. McKay offered the museum's apology for the original acquisition. The Rapa Nui community has indicated it will now turn its attention to the British Museum, which holds the moai *Hoa Hakananai'a*.

This is among the more substantial returns of Pacific ancestral remains so far, and it puts an Australian institution in front on a question many museums are still avoiding.

태평양 지역 조상 유해 반환 가운데 규모가 큰 편에 속하며, 여전히 많은 박물관이 미루고 있는 문제에서 호주 기관이 앞서 나간 사례입니다.
